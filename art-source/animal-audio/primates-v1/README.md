# Primate recordings v1

Six sources/ten excerpts cover gorilla, chimpanzee, orangutan, capuchin, ring-tailed
lemur and Japanese macaque. Exact original bytes, hashes, download links, edits,
licenses and decoded signal metrics are retained in provenance/manifest/recipe.
Public recording credits identify changes without implying creator endorsement.

Gorilla is an adult zoo display roar, played only on a fresh generic action pose.
Chimpanzee is a natural male pant-hoot excerpt from Fedurek et al.; it is explicitly
a phrase rather than a full call, also action-only. Neither emits idle roars.
Orangutan uses Lameira et al. natural male long-call grumbles, with sparse idle
playback and a seventy-second cooldown. No new courtship/calling AI is implied.

Japanese macaque uses two original recorded cooB examples from Supplemental Audio 2
of Furuyama et al. (2016). The paper identifies seven different recordings per
individual. These are not the manipulated cross-individual stimuli of Audio 3/4.
Capuchin is R. L. Araujo's Atlantic Rainforest family field recording, retaining
birds and forest background. Ring-tailed lemur retains group context. These voices
use sparse stationary calls; carrying stops capuchin playback and fruit work stays
quiet. Source sex/group qualifications do not imply new biological variants in art.

Freesound files retain exact public HQ MP3 previews, Commons files their original
distributed Ogg bytes, and orangutan the publisher's original WAV supplementary
recording. Source descriptions, waveforms/spectra and output signal limits were
inspected; **owner listening review remains pending**, particularly for background
noise. Mono 24 kHz, species filters, RMS target 0.16/source peak ceiling 0.75,
quiet edges and Vorbis quality 4; no artificial pitch variants. Builds consume
frozen outputs without downloading or re-exporting. Sources:
