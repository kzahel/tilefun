# Further calls and bill percussion v1

Four sources/seven excerpts cover kiwi, kakapo, shoebill and impala. The retained
sources, creator/license links, hashes, ranges, processing and decoded metrics are
explicit. Builds consume frozen OGG files without downloading or re-encoding.
Source identity, waveforms and decoded signal limits were checked; **owner
listening review remains pending**.

Kiwi uses Department of Conservation (NZ)'s male North Island brown kiwi
whistles; kakapo uses its adult male chinging source, not pitched-up booms or bill
percussion. The selected excerpts are short real phrases, not full calling bouts.
Original distributed WAV files are retained. Crown Copyright / Department of
Conservation (NZ) notices and required attribution are preserved in public credits.

Shoebill uses Peter Handke's CC0 bill-clattering video. Its complete original
Vorbis audio stream is retained in a small OGG by stream copy, without audio
re-encoding or unnecessary video payload. `originalMedia` pins the original
58,094,481-byte download and decoded PCM. `sourcePreparation` gives the exact
stream-copy command; decoded mono 48 kHz float PCM was verified byte-identical
between video and retained audio. The brief bill-clatter excerpt is explicitly
**non-vocal**. It adds no clattering animation or greeting/nesting AI.

Impala uses male rutting calls recorded in Tanzania by G de Courtivron with
Tascam DAP-1 and Sennheiser ME66, uploaded by roubignolle. Both are credited. The
retained Freesound HQ MP3 preview is not described as a lossless original. The
male/courtship source contexts of kiwi, kakapo and impala remain explicit; sparse
stationary playback adds no sex, mating or circadian AI. Calls remain quiet
through movement, feeding and unrelated work.

Species filters, mono 24 kHz, RMS target 0.16 limited by source peak 0.75, quiet
edges and Vorbis quality 4. Exported OGGs contain one audio stream. Field
background and perceived balance remain listening-review concerns.
