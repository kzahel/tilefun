# Dire wolf native observations

Observer: primary agent. This is source/visual QA, not human approval. Pending.

The four-facing native scenery shows a lean grey canid with a long dark muzzle,
pointed ears, raised shoulder ruff and a long bushy tail. Front and back are
narrower than the two profile views as expected from the fixed camera. The grey
feet and pale shoulder highlights remain separate against the grass. The native
body is larger than the Explorer but substantially smaller than the T. rex.

The walk has alternating planted paws, moving hips, body rise and head follow.
The independent saved-scene audit checks fixed limb lengths, ground contacts,
travel compensation and projected torso/head motion in every facing. The howl
raises the muzzle; charge stretches the stride and feeding lowers the head.
The tail is segmented source geometry and follows the pelvis, never a detached
screen-space ornament. Tiny facial pixels and far paws merge in front/back at
native size; this is a deliberate pixel-scale limit, not a claim of extra detail.
