# T. rex fierce-v1 — primary-agent observations

Pending human art review. Inspected native four-facing scenery alongside Explorer
and the retained elephant, plus chronological 3× walk and jaw-display strips.
The profile reads as a green biped dinosaur: large forward skull, visible teeth,
tiny arms, muscular hindquarters and long tapered balancing tail. Front/rear
views are foreshortened by the fixed wildlife camera; the rear includes much
more projected tail than the front. Native idle heights are front 60px, rear
97px and profiles 51px, compared with the small Explorer at the same density.

Walk inspection shows alternating hind-foot lift and travel, a moving pelvis
and chest, body pitch and stabilized head follow. The planted contact positions
are held against declared root travel in source; no whole-image translation is
used to imitate weight. Charge is its own longer-stride source sequence, rather
than a renamed copy of the old cats' walk. Jaw display opens the mouth, raises
the head and balances the tail. Feeding pitches the head forward; crouched rest
lowers the body while retaining grounded feet.

Independent saved-scene checks cover all 164 source poses, 984 fixed-length link
checks, 272 planted contacts and 104 stance/travel compensation checks. Projected
walk body motion remains 2.76–2.98px peak-to-peak across the four facings; head,
shoulder and pelvis also move by more than one native pixel. The nine-color
finish has binary alpha and clears its cell boundary in every pose.

Limitations: this is stylized rigid-volume art with coarse native toes and teeth,
not measured dinosaur biomechanics or a runtime skeletal model. The fixed camera
intentionally compresses head depth and overlaps the far leg. Scenery provides
scale context, not proof that every environmental composition has human approval.
The gameplay roar is synthesized presentation audio; the authority display and
wind-up determine when hunting begins. Human approval remains pending.
